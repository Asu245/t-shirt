# Competitor and audience research — Min

**Research question:** What can three established T-shirt retailers teach Min about product presentation, brand character, audience needs, and honest persuasion?

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-23  
**Project:** BRAND-001 / Min  
**Status:** Desk research. Findings below describe publicly available page content inspected on the review date; they are not customer research, interviews, usability results, or proof of customer demand.

## Method and limits

I reviewed the official US T-shirt collection or fit/product pages linked below. I recorded the visible organization, product information, and messaging. Retail pages are dynamic: products, prices, promotions, and availability can change. The access date is recorded instead of treating a page's current content as permanent. No customer interviews or competitor usability tests were conducted. A source page is evidence of what that retailer publishes, not independent proof that every marketing claim is true.

## Competitor comparison

### 1. UNIQLO — practical everyday basics

- **Original source:** [Men's T-shirts — UNIQLO US](https://www.uniqlo.com/us/en/men/tops/t-shirts)
- **Source type:** Official retailer collection page.
- **Date checked:** 2026-11-23.
- **Observed page content:** The collection groups products by types such as short sleeve, long sleeve, AIRism Cotton, cotton, and DRY. The retrieved listing included a range of sizes and product-level prices, ratings, and material labels for individual products.
- **What this suggests:** UNIQLO makes a large basics assortment easier to navigate by use or product family. Product-level information helps a shopper compare practical options.
- **Evidence vs. interpretation:** The category labels and displayed product information are observations. The idea that this organization reduces decision effort is an interpretation, not a measured result.
- **Possible Min application:** Keep the first product page focused on one shirt and make the important choice (size) easy to find. If Min later offers variants, organize them by a meaningful customer difference rather than decorative categories.

### 2. COS — design-led fit and silhouette

- **Original sources:** [Men's T-shirts — COS US](https://www.cos.com/en-us/men/t-shirts) and [Men's T-shirt fit guide — COS US](https://www.cos.com/en-us/fit-guide/men/t-shirt-fit-guide).
- **Source type:** Official retailer collection and fit-guide pages.
- **Date checked:** 2026-11-23.
- **Observed page content:** The collection separates fits such as slim, regular, relaxed, and oversized. The fit guide describes the shape through the body and sleeves and gives styling context. Individual listings include fabric and product details; the collection presents T-shirts as versatile wardrobe foundations.
- **What this suggests:** Fit is a meaningful product decision, and concise descriptions can help people understand the difference between silhouettes before choosing.
- **Evidence vs. interpretation:** Fit labels and descriptions are observed page content. The idea that they help shoppers feel more confident is a design hypothesis, not a measured result.
- **Possible Min application:** If Min receives verified garment measurements and fit information, add a small fit-and-measurements section. Until those facts are supplied, do not invent a fit label or imply that the sample shirt has a particular cut.

### 3. Everlane — materials and product transparency

- **Original sources:** [Men's tees and tanks — Everlane](https://www.everlane.com/collections/mens-tees-and-tanks), [The Essential Organic Crew — Everlane](https://www.everlane.com/products/mens-essential-organic-crew-uniform-white), and [Midweight Tee — Everlane](https://www.everlane.com/products/mens-classic-tee-white).
- **Source type:** Official retailer collection and product pages.
- **Date checked:** 2026-11-23.
- **Observed page content:** The collection offers categories including Essential, Archive, Midweight, Waffle, and Pique. Product pages provide fit descriptions, size guidance, material composition, care instructions, and—in applicable product listings—certification details.
- **What this suggests:** Specific product facts can do more persuasive work than broad claims such as “premium” or “sustainable.” A visitor can inspect what a garment is made from and how it is described.
- **Evidence vs. interpretation:** The named categories and listed product details are observations. The conclusion that this supports trust is an interpretation, not a measured result.
- **Possible Min application:** Add a product-facts area only when Min has verified composition, care, fit, and sourcing information. Keep unknown details explicitly unknown rather than borrowing another brand's claims.

## Two possible positions for Min

### Position A — dependable everyday essential

- **Promise direction:** A straightforward shirt for everyday situations.
- **Character:** Friendly, calm, practical.
- **Look:** Warm neutrals, clear product image, simple navigation, direct copy.
- **Persuasion:** Explain what the visitor can see and choose; keep size and price information clear.
- **Strength:** Easy to understand and close to Min's existing “reliable” promise.
- **Risk:** Many basics retailers already occupy this broad territory, so Min may feel generic without a more specific point of view.

### Position B — thoughtful modern minimalism

- **Promise direction:** An uncomplicated everyday shirt presented with care and clarity.
- **Character:** Calm, confident, considered.
- **Look:** Restrained typography, intentional spacing, warm neutral palette, strong hierarchy, product-first photography.
- **Persuasion:** Use accurate fit/material facts when available and explain useful choices without hype.
- **Strength:** Builds on Min's existing modernist direction and warm visual system.
- **Risk:** Minimal presentation can become vague if product facts and the visitor's next step are hard to find.

**Research recommendation:** Position B is the stronger direction to test because it connects the existing modernist visual decision with more specific product communication. This is a recommendation, not a recorded learner decision. Confirm or revise it with the learner before treating it as the final brand position.

## Two persona hypotheses

These are deliberately short design hypotheses. They are not real people, demographic profiles, or evidence from interviews.

### Hypothesis 1 — practical minimalist

- **Situation:** Wants a simple shirt that is easy to choose for an ordinary day.
- **Goal:** Find a straightforward option without sorting through unnecessary detail.
- **Possible hesitation:** Unsure about size or whether the product page gives enough information.
- **Page should help by:** Showing the product clearly, explaining the available sizes, and making the next action obvious.
- **Evidence status:** Assumption; not validated.

### Hypothesis 2 — design-conscious shopper

- **Situation:** Wants an understated shirt but pays attention to presentation, silhouette, and details.
- **Goal:** Understand the design and whether the garment suits the intended use.
- **Possible hesitation:** A minimal page may look attractive but omit useful product facts.
- **Page should help by:** Pairing a coherent visual identity with specific, verified product information.
- **Evidence status:** Assumption; not validated.

**Recommended audience to test first:** The design-conscious shopper, with the practical minimalist as a contrasting secondary hypothesis. This recommendation is consistent with Min's existing modernist visual direction, but it remains provisional until the learner confirms it and obtains real feedback.

## Connection to Min's current decisions

The current [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) records Min's name, Innocent archetype, calm/reassuring/reliable voice, warm minimal styling, and the promise “Just good old reliable for when you don't know what to wear.” This research does not replace those learner-approved decisions.

The existing storefront already includes:
- A visible size selector and demo-bag interaction.
- A clearly labeled fictional demo price.
- A free styling suggestion.
- A statement that no product claims have been supplied.

Those are useful foundations for honest persuasion. The styling suggestion offers a simple idea without claiming a product benefit that has not been verified. The page should continue to avoid invented reviews, scarcity, fabric composition, sustainability, fit, durability, or sales claims.
